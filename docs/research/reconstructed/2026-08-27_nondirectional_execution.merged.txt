P  Epistemic Governance, Statistical Validation Protocols, and Non-Directional Execution Mechanics in Decentralized Finance
P  Statistical Protocol Adaptation for Non-Sharpe Paired Objectives
P  When quantitative strategy optimization searches across discrete algorithmic parameterizations, evaluating candidates against a common reference on identical price paths requires statistical testing tailored to paired difference metrics rather than unconditional asset returns1.
P  Adapting Deflated Sharpe Ratios to Paired Basis-Point Statistics
P  The Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) addresses multiple-testing inflation and non-normality by adjusting the significance threshold as a function of the number of trials and the variance across candidate estimators3. In execution benchmarking, the metric of interest is the paired basis-point differential per cycle:
P  {d_t = r_cand,t - r_ref,t}
P  For a sample of {T} cycles, the sample mean differential is {d_bar = (1/T) sum_{t=1}^T d_t}, the sample standard deviation is {s_d = sqrt( (1/(T-1)) sum_{t=1}^T (d_t - d_bar)^2 )}, and the sample skewness and kurtosis are denoted by {gamma_hat_3} and {gamma_hat_4} respectively5. The standardized paired signal-to-noise ratio is defined as:
P  {theta_d = d_bar / s_d}
P  Under arbitrary non-normal difference distributions, the asymptotic variance of the estimator {theta_hat_d} follows the Mertens formulation6:
P  {sigma^2_{theta_d} = (1/T)(1 - gamma_hat_3 theta_hat_d + ((gamma_hat_4 - 1)/4) theta_hat_d^2)}
P  When selecting the maximum performer {theta_hat_max = max_{i=1,...,N} theta_hat_{d,i}} across {N} evaluated machines, the expected maximum under the global null hypothesis of zero true edge ({E[d_{i,t}] = 0} for all {i}) is governed by Extreme Value Theory (EVT) for the supremum of Gaussian limit processes6:
P  {E[max_{i=1,...,N} theta_hat_{d,i}] ~ sqrt(V[theta_hat_{d,i}]) ((1-gamma)Phi^-1(1-1/N) + gamma*Phi^-1(1-1/(Ne)))}
P  where {gamma ~ 0.57721566} is the Euler–Mascheroni constant, {Phi} is the standard normal cumulative distribution function, and {V[theta_hat_{d,i}]} is the cross-sectional variance of the trial statistics6.
P  The Deflated Paired Metric (DPM) computes the probability that the observed maximum differential reflects a true positive rather than selection-induced noise:
P  {DPM = Phi( (theta_hat_max - E[max_{i=1..N} theta_hat_{d,i}]) / sqrt((1/T)(1 - gamma_hat_3 theta_hat_max + ((gamma_hat_4-1)/4) theta_hat_max^2)) )}
P  While the EVT correction provides an efficient analytical filter during high-throughput strategy enumeration, its reliability degrades when candidate strategies exhibit dense cross-correlations6. For rigorous validation, Hansen's Superior Predictive Ability (SPA) test or the Romano-Wolf stepdown procedure is applied to the paired difference matrix1.
P  Hansen's SPA standardizes the loss differentials and re-centers the bootstrap distribution exclusively over models with non-positive historical performance, ensuring that candidate pools saturated with sub-optimal strategies do not artificially diminish statistical test power2.

P  Combinatorially Symmetric Cross-Validation Under Sample Constraints
P  Combinatorially Symmetric Cross-Validation (CSCV) estimates the Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) by partitioning {T} observations into {S} contiguous temporal blocks and constructing all combinations of training sets {J} and testing sets {J^c} of size {S/2}, yielding {C(S, S/2)} splits10. The strategy {i*} maximizing in-sample performance {R_i(J)} is evaluated out-of-sample to compute its relative percentile rank {omega_{i*}(J^c) in (0,1]}10. The logit of the rank generates the empirical distribution of relative performance:
P  {lambda = ln(omega_{i*}/(1-omega_{i*})),  PBO = integral_{-inf}^{0} f(lambda) d_lambda}
P  When empirical window counts are constrained ({T in [166, 375]}), the parameterization of {S} directly influences test reliability10:
P  Partition Dimension Selection ({S}): Configuring {S = 10} produces {C(10,5) = 252} splits, providing {16} to {37} observations per slice. Configuring {S = 16} yields {C(16,8) = 12,870} splits, but reduces slice size to {10} to {23} observations10. Slices with fewer than {25} observations suffer from severe within-slice sampling variance, which dominates the signal and drives PBO toward {0.50} regardless of true underlying structure10. For {T < 400}, {S = 10} is the minimum stable configuration.
P  Inadmissibility of Overlapping Windows: Generating synthetic overlapping windows introduces an artificial moving-average error structure {MA(q)} where {q} equals the step overlap10. This violates the assumption of block exchangeability10. Overlapping slices leak state variables between training and test sets, causing spurious cross-slice covariance that artificially depresses PBO11. Temporal partitions must be strictly contiguous, non-overlapping, and separated by an embargo window exceeding the strategy's autocorrelation horizon13.
P  Mechanics of Selection Generalization Under Level Collapse
P  The empirical finding where selection ranks generalize ({PBO in [0.05, 0.20]}) while absolute returns collapse ({+2.5 ... +16.8} bps in-sample to {-6 ... +2} bps out-of-sample) reflects rank preservation under negative structural drift, also known as frictional dominance or capacity exhaustion10.
P  This occurs when the strategy search isolates the parameterizations that most effectively capture relative microstructure dynamics, but the entire candidate distribution operates below the venue's fixed transactional friction10.
P  Let the gross expected edge of candidate {i} on path {t} be {g_i(X_t)} and execution friction be {c_t} (including base fees, tip overhead, and adverse selection)16. Observed net performance is:
P  {r_{i,t} = g_i(X_t) - c_t}
P  If {g_i(X_t) = alpha_i + epsilon_{i,t}} with fixed relative hierarchy {alpha_1 > alpha_2 > ... > alpha_N}, the relative ranking is preserved across all validation folds because {argmax_i(r_{i,t}) = argmax_i(g_i(X_t))} is invariant to uniform shifts in {c_t}10. However, if the maximum gross capacity satisfies {max_i alpha_i < E[c_t]}, the net return {E[r_{i,t}] < 0} across all candidates10.
P  CSCV confirms that the search identifies true relative optima within the defined model space, but it does not guarantee that the strategy space itself possesses positive economic expectation net of real-world friction1.
P  Statistical Power and Pre-Run Execution Gates
P  Evaluating sub-basis-point effect sizes without prior power validation guarantees high rates of Type II errors (false negatives), resulting in wasted computational cycles and inconclusive trials5.
P  Analytical Sample Size Derivations
P  Let {delta} represent the Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) in basis points, {alpha} the Type I error rate, and {1 - beta} the statistical power18.
P  For a paired experimental design with difference standard deviation {sigma_d}, the required sample size {N_paired} is:
P  {N_paired = (z_{1-a/2}+z_{1-b})^2 sigma_d^2 / delta^2}
P  For an unpaired two-sample comparison with per-group standard deviation {sigma_u}, the required sample size per group {n_group} and total sample size {N_unpaired} are:
P  {n_group = 2(z_{1-a/2}+z_{1-b})^2 sigma_u^2 / delta^2,   N_unpaired = 2 * n_group = 4(z_{1-a/2}+z_{1-b})^2 sigma_u^2 / delta^2}
P  For standard significance ({alpha = 0.05 ==> z_0.975 ~ 1.95996}) and power targets ({80% ==> z_0.80 ~ 0.84162}; {90% ==> z_0.90 ~ 1.28155}), the critical scaling factors are {7.84886} and {10.5074} respectively18.

P  {N_unpaired,total(0.25 bps, 80%) = 4 * 7.84886 * (6.6)^2 / (0.25)^2 = 21,882 cycles}

P  Non-Directional Execution Frameworks and Objective Redesign
P  Directional alpha in liquid crypto pairs decays within {50 ... 500} milliseconds, rendering minute-horizon price forecasting unprofitable after accounting for exchange and automated market maker (AMM) fees10. Strategy search must pivot from speculative directional bets to non-directional optimal execution and structural urgency extraction15.
P  Microstructure-Grounded Implementation Shortfall Minimization
P  The Almgren–Chriss framework provides a foundation for executing a total position size {X_0} over discrete steps {k in {1,...,K}} without directional forecasting22. The objective minimizes expected implementation shortfall (the difference between the initial arrival value and final executed value) subject to a variance penalty representing inventory risk15:
P  {min_{n_k} E[IS] + lambda V[IS]}
P  where {n_k} is the trade volume executed at step {k}, with {sum_{k=1}^K n_k = X_0}, and {lambda >= 0} parameterizes risk aversion22.
P  When translated to Decentralized Exchanges operating Constant Function Market Makers (CFMMs, {x * y = k}) and Concentrated Liquidity Market Makers (CLMMs), price impact dynamics are deterministic functions of pool reserves22:
P  Permanent Price Impact: Selling {n_k} tokens into a CFMM alters the pool's marginal reserve ratio permanently:{dP_k^perm = P_k (1 - x^2/(x+n_k)^2) ~ P_k (2 n_k / x)}
P  Temporary Impact and Pool Replenishment: Temporary impact arises from transient local liquidity depletion, which decays at an empirical replenishment rate {rho} as arbitrageurs rebalance pools against external centralized and decentralized venues21.
P  Dynamic Clip Scheduling: For retail-to-mid-size exits, the optimal schedule balances the convex price impact of large single orders against the cumulative base transaction fees and priority tips of multi-clip executions15.
P  The objective function replaces "edge versus hold" with Arrival Price Implementation Shortfall15:
P  {L_execution(pi) = sum_{k=1}^K n_k (P_0 - P_tilde_k) + sum_{k=1}^K Fee_k}
P  where {P_tilde_k} is the effective execution price of clip {n_k}, and {Fee_k} encapsulates base network fees, pool LP swap fees, and validator priority tips19. The search space enumerates deterministic execution trajectories {pi = {n_1, n_2, ..., n_K}} across routing venues to minimize {L_execution}, preserving the enumerate-and-verify harness without requiring directional forecasting15.
P  Risk-Centric, Drawdown, and Viability Formulations
P  When the operational objective is capital preservation and downside containment, optimization must prioritize tail-risk metrics3:
P  Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR / Expected Shortfall): Evaluates the mean loss beyond the {(1 - alpha)}-quantile of the execution shortfall distribution:{CVaR_alpha(L) = (1/alpha) integral_0^alpha VaR_u(L) du}This penalizes strategies that achieve acceptable average fills but experience catastrophic execution drag during sudden pool illiquidity or sandwich extraction19.
P  Minimax / Tropical Worst-Case Shortfall: Minimizes the maximum paired shortfall across all rolling historical sub-windows {W}:{min_theta max_w ( max_t V_ref,t - V_theta,t )}
P  Viability Sets: Computes the boundary of market states {(X_t, P_t, Depth_t)} from which the policy guarantees full liquidation within allowable slippage {S_bar} without triggering collateral liquidation thresholds24.
P  These formulations remain compatible with paired out-of-sample validation by benchmarking shortfall variance reduction and tail containment against standard Time-Weighted Average Price (TWAP) execution baselines10.
P  Structural Alpha in Non-Discretionary Urgency Horizons
P  While unconstrained spot pairs exhibit rapid informational decay, exploitable structure persists at horizons where market mechanisms force non-discretionary liquidity demand20:
P  DeFi Lending Liquidation Boundaries: On lending protocols (e.g., Kamino, Solend), accounts approaching liquidation boundaries (Health Factor {-> 1.0}) experience deterministic liquidation cascades24. Algorithms that pre-emptively liquidate collateral before third-party liquidators trigger seize penalties save the protocol liquidation penalty of {500 ... 1,000} bps24.
P  Perpetual Funding Rate Snapshots: Perpetual funding settlements (e.g., Drift) occur at fixed hourly boundaries24. Substantial basis divergences between spot and perpetual indices generate deterministic, mean-reverting basis flows in a {60}-second window surrounding the funding timestamp20.
P  Derivative Expiration and Binary Settlement: Options and prediction markets approaching settlement timestamps display localized volatility spikes and elevated Volume-Synchronized Probability of Toxicity (VPIN), creating predictable microstructure dynamics as market makers widen spreads20.
P  Autonomous Research Integrity and Epistemic Governance
P  Autonomous agent-driven quantitative research systems face systematic degradation modes when optimizing without strict procedural constraints1. Without formal boundaries, self-directed loops accumulate unvalidated assertions, suffer from context drift, and overfit validation simulators3.
P  Degradation Dynamics in Autonomous Loops
P  Autonomous research systems exhibit four distinct operational failure modes:
P  Harness Overfitting (Goodhart Drift): The agent identifies simulator-specific artifacts (such as zero-slippage assumptions on small-cap tokens) and produces strategies that exploit the simulator rather than true market mechanics10.
P  Context Entrapment: Large Language Model agents optimize within localized operational premises without re-evaluating foundational assumptions28. Socratic interrogation from external supervisors breaks this entrapment by questioning core hypotheses rather than micromanaging code implementation28.
P  Epistemic Debt Accumulation: Hypotheses are merged into documentation, but subsequent refutations fail to deprecate dependent modules16.
P  Metric Degradation: Performance criteria shift across runs to maintain positive reporting narratives3.
P  To prevent these failure modes, the system must enforce strict hypothesis state transitions:
P  Proposed: The hypothesis is specified with its theoretical mechanism and parameter bounds.
P  Committed: The hypothesis, search grid, and validation plan are cryptographically committed before accessing data.
P  In-Sample Validated: The candidate space is trained and screened on initial partitions.
P  Confirmed or Refuted: Out-of-sample evaluation gates determine whether the hypothesis passes pre-registered statistical bars.
P  Superseded: Deprecated models and refuted claims are marked as inactive to prevent downstream reuse.
P  Verification of External Literature via Citation Testing
P  To eliminate implementation discrepancies and invalid assumptions when adopting external quantitative research, the codebase must enforce Citation Tests18. A Citation Test is an automated unit test that encodes published mathematical relationships and validates implementation correctness against analytical benchmarks before merging external findings18:
P  Analytical Reproduction: Tests verify that power and sample size functions reproduce published analytical tables (e.g., confirming {N = 54} achieves {80%} power at {delta = 1.0} bps for {sigma_d = 2.6} bps)18.
P  Parameter Consistency Checks: Tests assert exact convention handling, distinguishing between one-tailed vs. two-tailed tests, pooled vs. unpooled variance, and per-group vs. total sample sizes ({N_total = 2 * n_group})18.
P  Synthetic Null Calibration: Continuous Integration (CI) runners execute synthetic Monte Carlo paths to verify that implemented statistical tests maintain nominal Type I error rates under null distributions2.

P  Economic Capturability of Fragmented Pool Spreads
P  The {27} bps clip-size spread observed on long-tail tokens (e.g., BONK) is driven by the convex bonding curves of fragmented Constant Product Market Maker (CPMM) pools relative to concentrated tick distributions in CLMMs23. However, realizing this spread net of transaction costs is heavily constrained17:
P  Base Pool Swap Fees: Standard CPMM pools (e.g., Raydium AMM v4) charge a fixed fee of {25 ... 30} bps per swap24. Multi-hop routing across fragmented pools compounds this cost unless routed through dynamic fee tiers15.
P  Priority Fees and Tip Auctions: Landing transactions ahead of competing arbitrageurs during volatility spikes requires bidding in Jito or Nozomi priority tip auctions, which consume {10 ... 15} bps of trade notional17.
P  Quote-to-Block Execution Gap: Latency between quote generation, client signing, and block commitment ({50 ... 400} ms) exposes orders to price drift, inducing {5 ... 8} bps of negative slippage on public mempool routes19.
P  Net realizable margin is defined as:
P  {Net Margin = Spread_gross - (Fee_pool + Tip_priority + Drift_latency + Fee_L1)}
P  Evaluating an empirical {27.0} bps gross spread on BONK:
P  {Net Margin = 27.0 bps - (25.0 bps + 10.0 bps + 5.0 bps + 0.2 bps) = -13.2 bps}
P  Without private transaction tunnels, zero-fee aggregation routing, or just-in-time on-chain simulation, transactional friction exceeds the gross spread, resulting in negative net realization17.

P  MiCA Article 78 Compliance and Verifiable On-Chain Audit Trails
P  Under the European Union's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation, Article 78 mandates strict Best Execution standards for Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs) executing orders on behalf of clients31:
P  Best Execution Mandate: CASPs must take all necessary steps to obtain the best possible result for clients, evaluating price, explicit and implicit costs, speed, likelihood of execution and settlement, size, and order characteristics31.
P  Regulatory Technical Standards (ESMA RTS): Regulatory standards mandate the maintenance of an immutable audit trail of all order and execution data for a minimum of five years31. Pre- and post-trade transparency data must be published within {30} seconds of execution (compared to {60} seconds under MiFID II)31.
P  Prohibition of Single-Broker Reliance: Routing client orders exclusively through a single internal market maker or unverified liquidity provider fails Article 78 compliance; CASPs must demonstrate multi-venue routing evaluation31.
P  A verifiable trade lifecycle—composed of an off-chain signed quote, an on-chain policy commitment PDA hash committed prior to execution, and the final fill transaction—provides a machine-auditable record for regulatory compliance34.
P  This architecture produces cryptographic proof that the routing algorithm selected the optimal execution path available across all accessible venues at timestamp {t}, directly fulfilling the regulatory requirements of Article 7831.

P  Integrated Operational Protocol
P  The research and execution pipeline operates through four discrete stages:
P  Hypothesis Registration and Power Gating: The agent registers target effect sizes ({delta}) and measured variance ({sigma_d}). The system calculates the required sample size ({N_paired}) and aborts execution if independent historical windows are insufficient ({T < N_paired})18.

P  Cross-Validation and Selection Screening: Candidate parameterizations are evaluated across non-overlapping, embargoed CSCV partitions. The top-performing machine must satisfy {DPM >= 0.95} and {PBO <= 0.20}4.
P  Confirmatory Out-of-Sample Testing: The selected machine is evaluated on locked out-of-sample data using Romano-Wolf stepdown controls against paired synthetic and historical null benchmarks2.
